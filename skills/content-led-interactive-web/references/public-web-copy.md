# Public Web Copy: Specificity Before Performance

Use this for visible public website copy: headlines, standfirsts, labels, calls to action, captions, feature explanations, section introductions, and public URL text. It is a writing quality check, not a brand voice or legal-review replacement.

## The Job Of A Sentence

Every consequential sentence should do at least one of these jobs for the intended reader:

- identify the subject, scope, or reader situation;
- make a concrete claim and name the mechanism, evidence, limitation, or distinction that earns it;
- orient the reader to what appears next; or
- enable a relevant action or decision.

If it does none of these, it may be atmosphere. Keep it only when the dossier says that exact atmosphere supports a named brand-memory moment; otherwise cut it.

## Four Tests

### 1. Substitution Test

Replace the subject, brand, product, place, or audience with an unrelated one. If the sentence still sounds equally convincing, it is probably generic. Replace the verdict with the thing that makes the sentence specific: object, reader, mechanism, limitation, timeframe, evidence, action, or trade-off.

### 2. Deletion Test

Delete the sentence. If the reader loses no fact, orientation, action, or distinctive stance, remove it. Do not preserve a line solely because it sounds polished.

### 3. Contrast-Proof Test

Patterns such as “not A but B”, “no A, only B”, and “more than A, it is B” are **not banned**. Keep one only if:

1. A and B describe materially different things;
2. the sentence says what changes in practice; and
3. the surrounding copy supplies evidence, scope, or an observable consequence.

If B simply repeats A with grander vocabulary, write the useful claim directly instead. Avoid stacking several reversal sentences in the first screen or a closing paragraph; their rhythm quickly becomes formulaic.

### 4. Evidence And Scope Test

Replace vague approval language — for example “proven”, “solid”, “confidence-building”, “ready to implement”, “built to last”, or “useful every year” — with the specific quality being claimed. State what works, for whom, under which condition, and how a reader can tell. Never invent metrics, customer proof, authority, or permanence merely to make copy sound certain.

## Open Directly, Then Earn Feeling

Lead with the topic and reader value when the reader needs orientation. A scene, image, or emotional hook may come first only when it already contains the subject's concrete tension, setting, or consequence. Do not open with a transferable mood that could introduce any category, then delay the actual point.

Feeling is allowed. The test is whether the feeling belongs to this subject: a particular place, friction, choice, memory, material, reader action, or proved brand cue.

## Revision Moves

| Weak move | Rewrite toward |
|---|---|
| Abstract verdict | Named criterion, mechanism, or observable result |
| Generic hook | Specific reader situation and subject |
| Reversal with no difference | Direct claim plus its consequence |
| Repeated promise | One primary promise, then evidence or a next action |
| Decorative superlative | A concrete distinction the reader can verify |

## Fast Final Check

For every headline, CTA, and first paragraph, ask:

1. Could this belong unchanged to an unrelated site?
2. What new fact, orientation, or action does it give the reader?
3. If it uses contrast, what specifically changes between the two sides?
4. Does its confidence come from content, or from rhythm and adjectives alone?

Do not force every sentence through every test. Apply the strongest test to the risk: substitution for generic brand prose, contrast proof for rhetorical turns, and evidence/scope for consequential claims.
